Git cheat sheet

The ideas behind the commands, three comparisons that trip everyone up, and every command LearnGit understands, with a level to practise each one.

Key ideasMerge or rebaseReset or revertFetch or pullCommands

Four ideas explain almost everything

Once these click, most Git commands are ways of moving labels around.

A commit

A saved snapshot of the project. It never changes, and it points back to its parent, so history is a chain you can walk backwards.

mainA branch

A movable label on one commit. Committing moves the label forward. Creating a branch copies nothing; it just adds a label.

HEAD

Where you are. Usually HEAD points at a branch. If it points straight at a commit, that is a detached HEAD.

origin/mainorigin

The shared copy of the repository on a server. origin/main is your bookmark of where its main was the last time you fetched or pushed.

Merge or rebase?

Both bring feature's work together with main's. They differ in the history they leave behind.

Rule of thumb: rebase your own unshared branch to tidy it up; merge when the branch is shared with others.

Reset or revert?

Both undo C3. Only one of them is safe once teammates have the commit.

Fetch or pull?

A teammate pushed C2 to origin. Both commands download it. Only one of them changes your branch.

Rule of thumb: fetch when you want to look before anything changes; pull when you are ready to bring the work in. If a push is rejected, pull first.

Commands

Refer to commits by branch or tag name, by ID such as C2, or relative to HEAD: HEAD~2 is two commits back, main^ is main's parent and HEAD@{1} is where HEAD was one move ago.
